Key finding — Brookings Apr 2026
Average tenure of retiring Republicans has dropped to just 5 terms — the lowest in four decades. In contrast, retiring Democrats average 9.9 terms. The historical majority-party average is 8.3 terms. This signals institutional frustration beyond mere retirement age — younger, less-established members are leaving.
Source: Brookings Institution "House retirement wave signals deep institutional frustration," Apr 2026 (Figure 2). Majority-party defined as the party holding the House majority at time of that Congress.

119th Congress — CRS R48535
The 119th Congress (2025–26) has a median Senate age of 64 and median House age of 58. The average (mean) age is 63.9 for Senators and 57.9 for Representatives — making it the third oldest Congress since 1789. This age factor has contributed to intra-party conflicts and prolonged institutional inertia despite clear party majorities.
Sources: CRS Report R48535 (119th Congress); Pew Research Center congressional age analyses (113th–119th); Quorum/LegiStorm historical data; Brookings Vital Statistics on Congress. 119th Congress — GovTrack
The 119th Congress has enacted only 2% of bills introduced, while making progress on only 9% of legislation. Two of the largest government shutdowns in US history (44-day all-department; 76-day DHS-related) occurred in this session. For context, the 95th Congress (1977–78) enacted 804 laws; the 117th Congress enacted 365; the 119th is on pace for fewer than 200.
Sources: CRS Report 96-727 (80th–108th Congress, 1947–2004). National Archives public law counts (109th–118th). GovTrack 119th Congress statistics (May 2026, partial session). Percentage = public laws enacted ÷ total bills & joint resolutions introduced.
