Key Policy Areas at Stake

How congressional control shapes the bilateral relationship
✈️

H-1B & High-Skilled Immigration

Roughly 70% of H-1B visas go to Indian nationals each year. Congress sets visa caps, employer fee structures, and the backlog clearance mechanism that leaves hundreds of thousands of Indian-born workers waiting decades for green cards.

Critical
📈

Trade & GSP Restoration

India was the largest beneficiary of the Generalised System of Preferences before its expiry in 2020. Renewed reciprocal trade tensions and tariff negotiations hinge on which party controls the House Ways and Means Committee.

Contested
🛡️

Defence Co-production & ITAR

The INDUS-X initiative, GE jet engine transfer, and future co-production deals require congressional notifications and ITAR waiver approvals. Armed Services Committee chairs in both chambers hold significant influence over the pace of these agreements.

Bipartisan
💻

Semiconductors & Technology

CHIPS Act implementation, AI governance frameworks, and export control regimes for advanced chips directly affect India's semiconductor ambitions and the commercial technology relationship. The Science and Commerce committees are central.

Watch
🌎

China Policy & the Quad

Both parties support the Quad framework and treat India as a counterweight to China. However, the depth of security guarantees, intelligence sharing agreements, and the pace of diplomatic engagement vary by administration and congressional posture.

Bipartisan
☀️

Climate & Clean Energy

US-India climate finance, the International Solar Alliance partnership, and critical minerals supply chains depend on whether the Inflation Reduction Act's international clean energy provisions survive congressional scrutiny in a divided government.

H-1B & Immigration: Legislative Tracker

Key bills in the 119th Congress affecting Indian nationals
H-1B and L-1 Visa Reform Act of 2025
Raises H-1B cap from 65,000 to 85,000; creates separate track for STEM graduates from US universities; tightens wage-level requirements to reduce undercutting. Key provision: spouse work authorisation (EAD) made permanent.
Lead sponsor: Rep. Zoe Lofgren (D-CA), co-sponsored by Rep. Ami Bera (D-CA)
In Committee
Equal Access to Green Cards for Legal Employment (EAGLE) Act
Eliminates the per-country cap on employment-based green cards, which causes Indian-born workers to wait 50–100 years for permanent residence under current law. Would immediately benefit the ~600,000 Indians in the EB backlog.
Introduced by Rep. Zoe Lofgren (D-CA) and Rep. John Curtis (R-UT)
Stalled — Senate
Immigration Innovation Act (I-Squared) 2025
Comprehensive high-skilled immigration reform; raises H-1B cap to 195,000; eliminates EB per-country caps; creates "V visa" for family members while awaiting green cards. Strong tech industry support.
Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN), Sen. Marco Rubio (R-FL) — bipartisan
Introduced — Bipartisan
America First Workforce Act
Restrictionist counterproposal: freezes H-1B cap, mandates higher prevailing wage requirements, restricts outsourcing firms' access to H-1B slots. Backed by Freedom Caucus and immigration restriction groups.
Rep. Bob Good (R-VA), Rep. Matt Gaetz (R-FL)
Counter-bill — Watch
Spouse Employment Protection Act
Permanently codifies the Obama-era rule allowing H-4 visa holders (dependants of H-1B workers awaiting green cards) to work. Currently under regulatory threat; legislative fix would protect ~100,000 workers, predominantly Indian women.
Rep. Pramila Jayapal (D-WA), Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL)
Stalled — House

Current Policy Landscape (2025–26)

Live developments in US-India relations shaping the 2026 calculus
Trade
Reciprocal Tariff Standoff
The Trump administration's 26% tariff on Indian goods (paused at 10% for 90 days) has complicated bilateral trade negotiations. India faces pressure to reduce its own tariffs in exchange for a US trade deal — a politically sensitive ask ahead of Indian state elections.
Active Negotiation
Technology
iCET 2.0: Initiative on Critical and Emerging Technology
The US-India iCET framework expanded in 2025 to cover quantum computing, AI safety standards, and space cooperation. CHIPS Act-linked semiconductor cooperation agreement signed; India reciprocated with liberalised FDI rules in chip manufacturing.
Active
Defence
INDUS-X & GE Jet Engine Deal
The GE F414 jet engine technology transfer for India's Tejas Mk2 fighter is proceeding through ITAR congressional notification. INDUS-X has spawned 11 joint defence startup partnerships. Armed Services Committees in both chambers are broadly supportive.
Progressing
Immigration
H-1B & EB Backlog: No Resolution in Sight
The employment-based green card backlog for Indian nationals exceeds 600,000. At current processing rates, a new applicant from India would wait 50–100 years. EAGLE Act has passed the House three times but stalled in the Senate due to per-country cap politics.
Critical
Quad
Quad Summit & Indo-Pacific Framework
The Quad (US, India, Japan, Australia) held its annual summit in 2025 with joint statements on semiconductor supply chains, maritime security, and clean energy. Congressional support is strong across both parties — one of few genuinely bipartisan foreign policy areas.
Bipartisan
Climate
US Withdrawal Risk from Climate Commitments
Republican control of Congress threatens IRA international clean energy provisions and US contributions to the Green Climate Fund, which India has leveraged for renewable energy financing. A Democratic House in 2027 would likely restore these mechanisms.
At Risk

★ A Historic Opportunity
No Indian-American has ever served in the United States Senate. The 2026 midterm cycle could change that: Ro Khanna (CA-17) and Raja Krishnamoorthi (IL-08) are both running for open Senate seats rated Safe D and Likely D respectively. A win by either would be the most significant milestone for the Indian-American community in US political history — comparable to Kamala Harris's 2010 California Attorney General win as a precursor to her eventual Senate and vice-presidential ascent.

Race Context
Khanna is running in a top-two primary field that includes Rep. Adam Schiff and Lt. Gov. Eleni Kounalakis. Policy Implications of an Indian-American Senator

What changes if Khanna or Krishnamoorthi reach the Senate
👔
Committee Positions on Immigration
A senator can sit on the Judiciary Committee and directly shape H-1B reform legislation. With an Indian-American in the room, EAGLE Act and H-4 EAD bills would have a powerful inside advocate with personal stakes in the outcome.
⚖️
Foreign Relations Access
Senate Foreign Relations Committee membership would give an Indian-American direct influence over bilateral agreements, ambassador confirmations, and India-specific hearings. No IA member has ever sat on this committee. Krishnamoorthi's HPSCI background makes him a likely Armed Services or Intelligence Committee pick.
🌟
Symbolic & Soft Power
A US Senator of Indian origin would be the most prominent Indian-American in US political history outside the Vice Presidency. It would significantly expand community fundraising capacity, mobilisation infrastructure, and political representation at the apex of the legislative branch.
📈
Trade & Technology Legislation
Senate Finance Committee (trade) and Senate Commerce Committee (technology) membership would give an IA senator direct leverage over GSP renewal, tariff schedules, and CHIPS Act implementation — all central to the US-India economic relationship. Khanna's tech background makes Commerce the natural fit.

Race Context
Ramaswamy, the biotech entrepreneur and 2024 Republican presidential candidate who served briefly as DOGE co-chair, entered the Ohio Governor's race as the Trump-endorsed candidate. Ohio has moved steadily toward Republicans — Trump won it by 11 points in 2024. DeWine is term-limited, leaving an open seat. If Ramaswamy wins, he would be the first Indian-American Republican governor in US history.
India Policy Angle
Ramaswamy's governorship would have limited direct bearing on US-India federal policy, but his national platform — built partly on anti-ESG and anti-DEI stances — has generated both enthusiasm and concern in parts of the Indian-American community. His Hinduism and opposition to certain immigration preferences (he has criticised H-1B dependency) make him an unusual figure in the diaspora political landscape.
  • Trump endorsement — dominant factor in Ohio Republican primary
  • Fundraising advantage from 2024 presidential campaign infrastructure
  • DOGE association: both asset (R base) and liability (independent voters)
  • India community views divided: pride vs policy concerns
