Members of Congress have been assigned to specific factions based on their caucus memberships, voting record, endorsements by leaders and broader public-ideological identity. On policy stances and political alignments, each of these factions is also determined based on the following points:

Traditional Republican / Establishment

  • Supports free trade and international alliances (like NATO)
  • Comfortable with corporate business interests and Wall Street
  • Prefers working within party leadership, not against it
  • Cautious about picking public fights with party leaders
  • Often has long ties to donor networks and business groups
  • Skeptical of isolationism
  • Oftentimes pro-Trump; criticism is private

MAGA Republican

  • Strongly and publicly loyal to Trump personally
  • Prioritises immigration crackdowns and border security
  • Distrusts mainstream media and federal agencies (FBI, DOJ)
  • Willing to challenge own party leadership publicly
  • Focuses on culture-war issues (schools, gender, “woke” topics)
  • Frequently uses combative, us-vs-them language

America First Republican

  • Publicly critical of the Trump administration
  • Sceptical of foreign wars and overseas military spending
  • Wants less U.S. involvement in international conflicts (like Ukraine)
  • Focuses on domestic manufacturing and jobs over global trade deals
  • Often overlaps with MAGA but stresses non-interventionism specifically
  • Wary of multinational institutions (UN, NATO funding)
  • Prioritises “America’s interests first” framing on trade and foreign policy

Moderate Democrat

  • Willing to work across the aisle with Republicans
  • Cautious about big government spending programmes
  • Supports incremental reforms, not sweeping overhauls
  • Represents swing districts, so avoids far-left labels
  • Less likely to support defunding police or Medicare for All
  • Focuses on pragmatic, “get things done” messaging

Progressive Democrat

  • Limited willingness to work across the aisle
  • Supports bold structural change (Medicare for All, Green New Deal)
  • Comfortable being vocal against corporate influence in politics
  • Prioritises racial and economic justice issues strongly
  • Often represents safe, deep-blue districts
  • Willing to criticise own party leadership publicly
  • Focuses on systemic reform over incremental fixes
