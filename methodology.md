# Methodology

The US 2026 Mid-term Election Observer is a research platform from DC Dossier and the Takshashila Institution. It tracks the 2026 elections for the House of Representatives, the Senate and state governorships, with a focus on what the results mean for India and India–US relations. This page explains where every number comes from, how often it updates and how we decide what to show.

## Our principles

We use public, citable sources only. Every chart and key figure on the site names its source and the date of the underlying data. Where data is older than our freshness limits, the site says so with a "May be outdated" tag. We correct errors openly and record them in the Corrections log below.

## Seats and boundaries

All 435 House seats, the 35 Senate seats on the 2026 ballot (33 regular seats plus special elections in Florida and Ohio) and the 36 governorships are tracked. Seats use one standard label throughout the site. House seats use the state and a two-digit district (for example AZ-01), Senate seats use "Senate" and the state (Senate-TX), and governorships use "Gov" and the state (Gov-GA).

Several states redrew their congressional maps before 2026. Where a state uses a new map, seats are shown on the 2026 boundaries. Elsewhere the boundaries used for the 119th Congress apply.

## Race ratings

Ratings come from three independent forecasters: the Cook Political Report, Sabato's Crystal Ball and Inside Elections. We use their seven-point scale, from Solid Democratic to Solid Republican. Ratings are reviewed by our team and updated by hand, and each chamber shows the date of its latest review. Ratings older than 14 days are flagged.

## Polling

Race polls and the generic congressional ballot come from the New York Times polling averages and the individual polls it lists. Polls are grouped by race, so a Senate poll is never averaged with a governor's poll in the same state. Each seat shows its five most recent polls and a 30-day average where enough polls exist. Polls older than 14 days are flagged.

## Approval and economic data

President Trump's approval rating comes from the Silver Bulletin average. Congressional approval comes from Gallup's monthly survey and is shown with the month the survey was conducted. Inflation comes from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics consumer price index, using the 12-month change in all items. Monthly figures older than 45 days are flagged.

## Retirements and departures

The count of members not seeking re-election comes from Ballotpedia's list of incumbents not running in 2026, split by chamber and party. Senators who are running for another office mid-term, and so keep their seat unless they win, are not counted as departures.

## Seat briefs

Each battleground seat has a brief written by the Takshashila research team. Briefs cover the seat overview, electoral context, key local issues, strategic outlook (including significance for India–US interests) and key local media. Briefs are researched from public sources, which are linked in the text, and are refreshed as races develop. Each brief shows its last update.

## India's Interest

This section tracks members of Congress whose work or constituencies bear on India–US relations, including all Indian-American members. Selection is based on committee roles, caucus membership, sponsored legislation and public statements. Inclusion describes relevance to India–US ties. It is not an endorsement.

## News

The news feed scans more than 150 public sources, including national outlets, state and local newspapers and election analysts. Each story is scored for relevance to the 2026 elections. Stories that name a tracked candidate or seat, or use clear election terms, score higher. Stories on unrelated subjects are removed. Only stories from the past seven days are shown, with no more than ten from any one source per update.

Candidate news is gathered separately by searching each tracked candidate's name. A story is shown only when the candidate's full name, or their title and surname, appears in the headline or summary.

## Analysis and Commentary

This section lists work by Takshashila researchers on the 2026 elections and India–US relations, including op-eds, newsletters, research documents, media appearances and podcasts. Every entry is selected by hand. Titles, dates and summaries are drawn from the original publication. Where a summary is written with AI assistance, it is reviewed before publication.

## How often data updates

News is checked roughly every few hours. Polls, approval, inflation and retirements are checked every four hours. Candidate news is checked every six hours. Ratings and seat briefs are updated by hand. Automated schedules can run later than planned, so always check the "Data as of" date shown with each figure.

## Intra-party factions

Faction labels for members of Congress are our own analytical groupings. They draw on voting records, including Heritage Action scorecards, caucus membership and public positions. They are a research aid and may change as members' records develop.

## Limitations

Forecasts and polls are estimates, not predictions of certain outcomes. Automated sources can fail or change without notice. When that happens, the site keeps the last good value and shows its date rather than displaying blank or incorrect data. Local news coverage varies by state, so the absence of news for a candidate does not mean the race is quiet.
